Before a kitchen remodel starts, settle the layout, cabinets, countertops, appliances, plumbing fixtures, lighting, and flooring. Know your lead times, plan where you will cook during construction, and confirm what permits your city or county requires. Decisions made before demo day keep the schedule tight and the budget predictable.

## Why does the pre-demo checklist matter?

Demolition is the point of no return. Once walls, cabinets, and flooring are out, every open question has a cost: the crew waits, a delivery slips, or a change order appears. Most kitchen schedule problems are not construction problems. They are decision problems that surfaced late.

A good contractor helps you front-load those decisions. Here is the checklist we walk through with homeowners.

## What layout decisions should be final?

Layout drives everything else, so start there.

- **Work triangle and traffic:** where you cook, clean, and store, and how people move through.
- **Walls and openings:** whether any walls move, which can involve structural review and permits.
- **Island or peninsula:** size, seating, clearances, and electrical needs.
- **Utility locations:** sink, range, refrigerator, dishwasher, and any gas lines.
- **Storage goals:** pantry, trash and recycling, small appliances, and tall storage.

Moving a sink or range is not impossible, but it adds plumbing, gas, or electrical work. Know that tradeoff before you commit to the plan.

## Which products need to be chosen first?

Choose products that affect dimensions or lead time before anything else.

### Appliances

Pick the exact models. Cabinet openings, ventilation, electrical, and gas requirements all follow from the appliance specs. A refrigerator that is an inch too wide is a cabinet problem.

### Cabinets

Style, finish, door and drawer configuration, hardware, and any custom elements. We work with showroom partners including InnoCraft Cabinetry, and our showroom is open by appointment for owners who want to see materials in person. Showroom customers receive discounts on materials.

### Countertops and backsplash

Material, edge profile, seam locations, and sink cutout. Countertops are typically templated after cabinets are installed, so the timing of that step affects your schedule.

### Plumbing fixtures and lighting

Faucet, sink, disposal, pot filler if any, recessed lights, under-cabinet lighting, pendants, and switch locations. Changing a light location after the drywall closes is expensive.

## What does a kitchen remodel decision timeline look like?

| Timing | What should be settled |
| --- | --- |
| During design | Layout, appliance models, cabinet style, countertop material |
| Before ordering | Finishes, hardware, fixtures, lighting plan, flooring |
| Before demo | Lead times confirmed, temporary kitchen set up, schedule agreed |
| During construction | Only small adjustments, approved in writing |

At Wayco, projects typically start within about four weeks, with design and submittals first, then material procurement, then mobilization. Selections made during week one and two keep that rhythm intact.

## How do you plan to live without a kitchen?

This is the part people forget. A kitchen remodel can run for weeks, and you still need to eat.

- Set up a temporary kitchen with a microwave, toaster oven, or hot plate, a small fridge, and a place to wash dishes.
- Move dishes, pantry items, and small appliances out of the work area before demo.
- Plan around water, gas, and power shut-offs. Ask which days those are.
- Protect floors and furniture in adjacent rooms. Dust travels.
- Talk through pets, kids, and work-from-home schedules with your project team.

## What permit and inspection questions should you ask?

Electrical, plumbing, gas, and structural changes usually involve permits and inspections, and requirements differ by city and county. Ask your contractor who pulls permits and who schedules inspections. Wayco handles all required permitting, so you are not chasing paperwork. ## What budget questions are worth asking now?

- What is included in the price, and what is excluded?
- Which items are allowances, such as tile or fixtures, and what happens if you go over?
- How are changes priced and approved?
- What is the warranty? Ours is a 1-year workmanship warranty.
- What is the payment schedule, and what milestones trigger payments?

Putting these in writing before demo avoids awkward conversations later. Our [kitchen remodeling](/kitchen-remodeling) team lays them out in a clear scope, and if your plan touches other rooms, our [whole-home renovation](/whole-home-renovation) service can coordinate the rest.

## Your final pre-demo checklist

1. Layout approved and drawn
2. Appliance models chosen and dimensions confirmed
3. Cabinets, counters, tile, and fixtures selected and ordered
4. Lead times confirmed against the schedule
5. Permits submitted or issued
6. Temporary kitchen ready
7. Written scope, price, and change-order process signed
8. Access, parking, and work hours discussed

## What should you do the week before demo?

The final week is about readiness, not decisions. Walk through the schedule with your project contact and confirm the first day of demolition, who will be on site, and where materials will be staged. Clear out cabinets and counters, and take down wall art in nearby rooms. Move anything fragile out of rooms that share walls with the kitchen.

Confirm that appliance and cabinet deliveries are scheduled and that someone can receive them. Take photos of the existing kitchen so everyone remembers the starting point. Finally, agree on how you will communicate during construction, whether by call, text, or a weekly meeting, so small questions get answered fast.
