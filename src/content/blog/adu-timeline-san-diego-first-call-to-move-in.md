An ADU in San Diego County typically takes several months to build once construction begins. The full timeline from first call to move-in also includes design, plan submittals, and permit review, which vary by jurisdiction. With Wayco Construction, most projects start within about four weeks of your first conversation.

## What does the ADU timeline look like from start to finish?

Think of an ADU project in four stages: getting started, design and permitting, construction, and closeout. Only the first has a fixed rhythm with us. The rest depend on your lot, your city or county, and the choices you make along the way.

### Weeks 1 to 2: design and submittals

After a free consultation and a look at your property, we finalize the layout and prepare submittals. Our turnkey packages include city and county pre-approved design plans and full engineering, which means we are not starting from a blank page. Wayco handles all required permitting, so you are not filling out agency forms on your own.

### Week 3: material procurement and deliveries

While plans move through submittal, we order and schedule materials. Early procurement is one of the quiet reasons projects start on time. Long lead items such as windows, cabinetry, and specialty fixtures get ordered while paperwork is still moving.

### Week 4: mobilization on site

In week four we mobilize: crew, equipment, temporary facilities, and the first site work. This is the start of the construction phase. It is not the end of permit review for every project, and we will tell you clearly where your jurisdiction stands.

## How long does ADU permit review take?

It varies by jurisdiction, and we will not guess. San Diego County is a patchwork of cities and unincorporated areas, each with its own staffing, queue, and checklist. California law generally supports ADU approvals and sets processing expectations, but the specifics depend on your location and your property. Check with your city or county for current guidance.

What we can control is how complete your submittal is. Clean plans, accurate site information, and quick responses to agency comments shorten the back-and-forth. Our [design and planning](/design-and-planning) work is built around that.

## How long does the construction phase take?

Ground-up ADU construction usually takes several months. Size matters, but so do site conditions, utility connections, weather, inspections, and how quickly finish selections are made. A garage conversion is often faster than a new detached unit because the structure exists, though we have to confirm its condition first.

Here is the general order of work:

- Site preparation and foundation
- Framing, roofing, and exterior envelope
- Rough plumbing, electrical, and mechanical
- Inspections at required stages
- Insulation, drywall, and interior finishes
- Cabinets, fixtures, appliances, and flooring
- Final inspections, punch list, and move-in

## What slows an ADU project down?

| Cause of delay | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| Incomplete or changing plans | Lock the layout before submittal |
| Agency review comments | Respond quickly; let your builder handle resubmittals |
| Unexpected site conditions | Get a site walk before the price is final |
| Utility coordination | Contact providers early, check requirements |
| Late finish decisions | Choose materials before construction begins |

The pattern is consistent. Projects slow down when decisions are made late or when no one owns the schedule. A single point of contact and a written schedule fix a lot of that.

## What can you do to keep your ADU on schedule?

You have more influence than you might think.

- Decide on layout and finishes early. Changes after submittal can restart review.
- Respond to questions within a day or two.
- Gather any site documents you have, such as a survey or utility information.
- Keep access to the build area clear.
- Ask for a written schedule and expect updates.

Clients tell us the thing they value most is knowing where the project stands. We keep communication regular and schedules clear for that reason.

## Is it faster to convert a garage?

Often, yes, though not always. A conversion skips the foundation and framing of a new building, but it brings its own questions about the slab, the roof, parking, and code requirements for a living space. We cover the tradeoffs in [our guide to ADU types](/blog/detached-attached-or-garage-conversion-adu), and you can learn more about [ADU construction](/adu-construction) with Wayco.

## What happens at the end of an ADU project?

The last stretch is often shorter on paper than it feels in person. After the interior finishes go in, the building has to pass final inspections, and a punch list covers small items such as touch-up paint, adjustments to doors, and fixture checks. Utility connections and final sign-offs from your city or county come before you can occupy the unit.

We walk through the finished ADU with you, explain how systems work, and review your 1-year workmanship warranty. Plan a little flexibility around your move-in date, especially if a tenant or family member is waiting. Inspections are scheduled by the agency, and we coordinate them as quickly as the process allows.
