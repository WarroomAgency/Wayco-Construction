Rebuilding after a house fire follows a sequence: secure safety, start the insurance claim, document losses, clear and assess the site, design the new home, obtain permits, and rebuild. Each step takes patience. A design-build contractor can keep design, permitting, and construction coordinated so you deal with one team while you recover.

## What do you do in the first days after a fire?

Safety and documentation come first.

- Make sure everyone, including pets, is safe and has a place to stay.
- Contact your insurance company as soon as you can and ask what they need.
- Do not enter the structure until authorities say it is safe.
- Take photos and video of what you can see from safe areas, and begin a list of lost items.
- Secure the property against weather and trespass, and keep receipts for emergency expenses.
- Ask your insurer about temporary housing coverage.

This is stressful, and you do not have to decide everything at once. Take things one task at a time.

## How does insurance work in a fire rebuild?

Policies differ, so read yours and ask questions. Coverage for the structure, contents, and additional living expenses may be handled separately. Your adjuster makes coverage decisions, but a contractor can help by:

- Walking the site and describing what rebuilding involves
- Providing a written scope and pricing
- Explaining code upgrades that may apply to a new build
- Keeping documentation organized for your claim

Be cautious with anyone who pressures you to sign quickly after a loss. Take time, verify licenses with the California Contractors State License Board (CSLB), and get everything in writing. ## What does the rebuild process look like?

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Safety and claim | Secure the site, open the insurance claim, document losses |
| 2. Assessment | Evaluate remaining structure, foundation, utilities |
| 3. Debris removal | Clear the site safely, following local requirements |
| 4. Design | Decide whether to match the old home or change it |
| 5. Permits | Submittals to your city or county |
| 6. Construction | Foundation work, framing, systems, finishes |
| 7. Closeout | Final inspections, walkthrough, move-in |

Debris removal and site clearing have specific rules, and requirements can vary by agency and by what burned. Check with your city or county, and ask your contractor to coordinate.

## Should you rebuild the same home or redesign?

You have two realistic paths.

**Rebuild as it was.** Familiar layouts and a simpler design phase. Current building codes may still require updates, so a rebuild is rarely an exact copy.

**Redesign.** An opportunity to fix what never worked: smaller kitchen, awkward flow, too few bedrooms, poor energy performance. A redesign can cost more, but it can also be the right moment to make choices you will live with for decades.

Some owners rebuild and add an ADU at the same time. California law generally supports ADUs on residential lots, and local rules vary, so check with your jurisdiction. Our [ADU construction](/adu-construction) team can help evaluate that option.

## How do you stay organized during a long rebuild?

- Keep one folder, digital and paper, for the claim, estimates, permits, and contracts.
- Ask for a written schedule and expect regular updates.
- Decide on finishes in batches so ordering does not stall construction.
- Keep a list of open questions for each weekly check-in.
- Plan for emotional ups and downs. Rebuilding is practical work, and it is also personal.

## What should you expect emotionally and practically?

A rebuild after a fire is not only a construction project. Families often deal with temporary housing, lost belongings, insurance paperwork, and decisions about a home they did not choose to replace. It is normal to feel overwhelmed. Break the work into small steps, and lean on people who can carry parts of it for you.

Practically, expect the early months to be mostly paperwork and planning, with visible progress on site coming later. That quiet phase is where good contractors earn trust, by explaining each step, keeping you informed, and handling permits so you do not have to. Ask for a written schedule and a regular check-in, even when there is little to report.
